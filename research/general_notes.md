# How does electrolysis work?
`` Electricity -> H2 ``

**Electrolyser:** `` 2H2O(l) → 2H2(g) + O2(g) ``
- Uses electricity to separate water molecules into H2 and O2 molecules
- Contains 2 electrodes and an electrolyte separating them
- Hydrogen at the cathode, oxyden at the anode

**Electrolyser loop:**
1. Cathode:
    - Water + electrons → H₂ + OH⁻
2. OH⁻ travels → anode
3. Anode:
    - OH⁻ → O₂ + electrons
4. Electrons flow through external circuit

**Cathode (-):** `` 2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻ ``
- Negative electrode where reduction takes place (gain of electrons)
- Water molecules gain electrons from the electric flow, forming h2 and hydroxide ions

**Anode (+):** `` 4OH⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ ``
- Positive electrode where oxidation takes place (loss of electrons)
- Negatively charged hydroxide ions move toward the anode
- Hydroxide ions give up electrons, oxygen and water are formed

**Main types of electrolysis:**
- Alkaline Electrolysis
    - Uses a liquid alkaline electrolyte
    - Low cost, slow but mature and proven technology
    - OH- ions move between the electrodes

# How does a fuel cell work?
`` H2 -> electricity ``
- Reverse Electrolysis
- H2 is not the fuel itself, it is merely the carrier of energy

**Components of a fuel cell:**
- H2 as input
- Anode
- Electrolyte membrane
- Cathode
- Wires

**Hydrogen:**
- Exists in h20 form mostly on our planet
    - Separated from water via electrolysis and steam reformation
- Extremely flammable and explosive - storage tank need to withstand high tolerances
- Needs to be compressed to 700 atmospheric pressure to reach the fuel density needed
- Fed to the fuel cell to undergo reverse-electrolysis to generate electricity


![Diagram of Fuel Cell](https://specialgasinstruments.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/hydrogen-fuel-cell-image.webp)


**Anode vs Cathode Electrodes:**
- Anode - where oxidation occurs (loss of electrons)
- Cathode - where reduction occurs (gain of electrons)
- Polarity:
    - Charging battery: anode is positive, cathode is negative
        - anode begins as the positive electrode, cathode the negative
        - catalyst is used to oxidise the h2 molecules, resulting in H+ ions and e- electrons
        - h+ ions then cross the electrolyte membrane

    - Discharging battery: anode is negative, cathode is positive
        - Positive H+ ions pass the membranes, leaving behind electrons in the anode, anode becomes negative
        - Excess of electrons in anode (negative) -> electrons flow from anode to cathode


**Outputs of the fuel cell:**
- **DC current** from electrons moving from the now negative anode to the now positive cathode
    - this DC current must be converted to AC with an inverter before used by homes
- **Water** as a byproduct when the H+ ions make contact with O2 from the atmosphere to form water at the cathode end
